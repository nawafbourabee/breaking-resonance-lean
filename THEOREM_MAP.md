# Coverage by manuscript statement

This table follows the supplied `rhmc_upper_lower (1).tex` and
`Breaking_Resonance (2).pdf` reviewed on 23 November 2026. The TeX SHA-256 is
`ae71ca5d490e17a95692fce64e5effa880727a869ee5629423a208159045477a`.
The manuscript is external to this Lean project.

| Manuscript statement | Lean declarations |
|---|---|
| Definition 2.1, non-resonant duration law | `NonresonantDuration` on a probability measure carried by $(0,\infty)$. The real characteristic function condition is expressed as the cosine integral. |
| Algorithm 2.1 and the position kernel | The measurable probability kernel, including the fair Bernoulli draw, momentum refresh and endpoint Metropolis decision, is modeled by `roundedVerletKernel`. The fixed-count and mixture formulas are `proposalKernel_apply` and `randomizedVerletKernel_mixture`. |
| Theorem 1, mixing from a warm start | Formalized by `theoremOne` for the manuscript assumptions (A1) to (A3), normalized position Gibbs law, and actual position kernel. The step size threshold is the explicit $c_1$. The uniform $c_2$ follows by replacing $p_S$ with $1-\delta_\rho/4$. The exact law-specific constants in (3.4) are checked by `theoremOne_explicit_normalized`. The proof checks the Gibbs tail and the scalar parameter substitutions. Its two cited inputs are `PaperTalagrand` and `PPInterpolation`. |
| Corollary 1, gradient complexity | The expectation of the rounded count and the fixed-length sum of counts, which give the gradient cost of the transitions in Theorem 1, are checked. |
| Theorem 2, necessity of Hessian regularity for fixed step sizes | Formalized by `theoremTwo`. It uses the product potential in (7.2) on the fast coordinates and the two auxiliary coordinates. It proves the optimal curvature constants, half-mass start, and gradient lower bound for the actual rounded Metropolis kernel and every duration law satisfying (2.17). The explicit constants are `lowerCostFirst` and `lowerCostSecond`. The gradient count is the expected sum of proposal counts, which is a lower bound on the full number of gradient evaluations. |
| Proposition A, mixing from restricted conductance (Section 4) | Formalized by `propositionA` for a general reversible Markov kernel with restricted conductance and the lower spectral inequality. The proof derives the weak Poincare inequality with the paper constants from the flow of upper level sets. It checks reversible density evolution, the variance recurrence, the total variation bound, and truncation and normalization of the $L^2$-warm start. |
| Proposition B, lower spectral bound (Section 5) | Formalized by `propositionB`, using the explicit Fourier choice for $\beta_P$ and the step size in the paper. It proves reversibility and the squared-norm inequality for the actual position kernel on all real $L^2(\pi)$. The more general `roundedVerlet_lower_spectral_of_fourier` allows any $\beta_P$ satisfying (3.1). Finiteness of the normalizer is part of the manuscript setting. |
| Proposition C, restricted conductance (Section 6) | Formalized by `propositionC`, with the energy shell, step-size condition, reference entropy and conductance, conditional count law, and Fourier transfer. The two constants are $\eta=c/600$ and $\phi_P=p_Sc/60$. The Gibbs law is written with the normalization of Section 6. |
| Lemma 5.1, reversibility | Formalized by `verlet_gradient_reversibility`, for $C^1$ potentials with finite Gibbs normalizer, fixed counts, and arbitrary state-independent mixtures. |
| Lemma 5.2, comparison of adjusted kernels | Formalized in quadratic-form notation by `randomizedVerletKernel_lower_l2` and `randomizedVerletKernel_comparison_l2`. Both results hold separately on all real $L^2(\pi)$. The forms are the iterated integrals for the actual position kernels. The characteristic function, accepted part and rejection form have their manuscript definitions. |
| Lemma 5.3, Fourier bounds after rounding | Formalized by `roundedFourierBounds`. It checks both bounds of (3.1), at every frequency in the stated interval, for the actual rounded conditional, full and triangular duration laws with the paper's explicit constants. |
| Lemma 5.4 and Appendix C, the three duration laws | Lemma 5.4 is formalized by `triangular_example_fourier`, `uniform_example_fourier`, and `exponential_example_fourier`. All three rows have the stated cutoffs, comparison constants, spectral constants, and retained probabilities. `triangularCount_eq_endpointLaw` identifies the reference count law with the endpoint mixture in Proposition 6.8. The uniform law is on $[0,Nh/2]=[0,2/\sqrt m]$ for the step size in the paper. `uniformHalfCount_cosine_ratio` verifies the printed even and odd formulas in Appendix C. |
| Lemma 6.1, modified energy | `ppModifiedEnergy_of_hessian` proves the identity and the cubic bound with the factor $1/12$ under (A3). The proposal proof takes the cited result explicitly as `PPModifiedEnergy`. |
| Lemma 6.2, energy control | The pointwise energy bounds and the flight velocity bound are checked, together with the modified-energy increment estimate used for the proposal. |
| Lemma 6.3, Talagrand | Explicit input `TalagrandBound`, exactly the inequality used in Proposition 6.8 for finite relative entropy. |
| Lemma 6.4, Persistence Pays interpolation | Explicit input `PPInterpolation`, with the paper's finite endpoint condition. The interpolation result checked in the Persistence Pays development is retained as a supporting declaration. |
| Lemma 6.5, relative entropy to a Gaussian | The sharp Gaussian covariance inequality used in the analysis is checked. |
| Lemma 6.6, free-transport estimate | The integrated bound for the current $\mathcal J(p_t)$ with coefficient two, continuity and the first variation are checked for twice continuously differentiable potentials, the setting of Theorem 1. |
| Lemma 6.7, weighted entropy estimate | `verlet_weighted_entropy_with_initial_entropy` proves (6.19) on the actual trajectory with the same constants for twice continuously differentiable potentials, the setting of Theorem 1, using Lemma 6.4 as an input. |
| Proposition 6.8, entropy of the proposal law | Formalized with the explicit cited inputs for Lemmas 6.1, 6.3 and 6.4. `verlet_triangular_proposal_entropy` uses the initial energy shell, finite entropy and centered momentum to establish the proposed position entropy bound. `triangularEndpointLaw_eq_triangularWeights` identifies its law with (5.4). All three coefficients agree. The $C^2$ requirement is part of (A3) here. The calculation uses the potential normalized at its minimizer, as in Section 6. |
| Lemma 6.9, entropy contraction implies restricted conductance | Formalized by `general_entropy_conductance` for the conditional refreshment kernel in the manuscript. Its composition product with the position marginal is the joint reference law. The proof uses the general $\theta,\delta_0,\phi_0$ and the stated entropy threshold. The existence of a measurable conditional refreshment kernel is implicit in (6.29) and explicit in Lean. |
| Lemma 7.1 and Section 7.2, return cone and expanding orbit | Formalized by `cycle_blocks`, `cycle_step` and `expandingVerletOrbit`. These check the strict signs and intermediate magnitudes, preservation of the cone, and the stated expansion factor for the actual velocity Verlet recurrence. The curvature bounds and the scalar force are checked in `LowerAngles.lean` and `LowerScalarPotential.lean`. |
| Lemma 7.2, a set of expanding trajectories | Formalized by `expandingSet_images_disjoint`, `expandingSet_energy_upper`, `expandingSet_gibbs_probability`, and `expandingSet_energy_lower`. The phase measure is the scalar position Gibbs law times standard Gaussian momentum. The probability and energy constants are exactly those in the statement. |
| Section 7.3, rejection cutoff | The product Gibbs law and its energy moment are checked by `separablePhaseGibbs_coordinates` and `separable_phase_exp_moment`. The exceptional-event estimate, energy increase and conditional acceptance bound are checked by `lowerBadPhase_bound`, `lowerEnergyIncrease` and `lowerEstimate_acceptance`. |
| Section 7.4, mixing and gradient lower bounds | The conditional acceptance estimate gives the two iteration lower bounds in `lowerIteration_count`. The proof of `theoremTwo` uses the exact expectation of the sum of rounded counts and the stated gradient constants. |
| Lemma A.1, regularity of the smoothed laws | The Gaussian smoothing, continuity, and finite-entropy results used in Section 6 are checked. |
| Proposition B.1, gradient-evaluation lower bound for Gaussian targets | Formalized by `gaussianGradientLowerBound` for positive step sizes with $h\sqrt{L}\le 1$, the diagonal Gaussian target, the halfspace start, and any independent count law of positive finite mean. It uses a polar integral and comparison of Gaussian densities for the scalar crossing bound. `markovPathLaw_exit_le_crossing_sum` proves the inclusion and union bound on the actual position path, and `gaussianExpectedGradients_eq` verifies the gradient count, with the initialization cost represented by its nonnegative mean. |

The manuscript cites Ambrosio, Gigli and Savare, Theorem 8.4.7, for first
variation and proves the continuous derivative it needs at the cell endpoints.
The current formalization uses the checked frozen-coupling proof
`freeTransport_positionCost_hasDerivAt`. It does not take an almost
everywhere derivative formula as an endpoint assertion.
